`qaren check` and `qaren pr` now look for another UI automation driver on the selected device right after they take the device lease, before dependencies, the native build and the launch. The check refuses only on proof: on iOS a UI-test runner whose kernel executable lies inside the selected simulator, or a host `xcodebuild` test or `maestro` command whose exact arguments target it; on Android a process with a known driver's exact name. A driver that a later complete scan does not show gone refuses `DEVICE_BUSY` with `failure.phase` `preflight` and `failure.subreason` `foreign-driver`; the check reads at most three answers, 2 seconds apart, and none later than 12 seconds after it began. The refusal names the driver by a class from a fixed list and, on iOS, its host process id; no process name, path or argument of a foreign driver is recorded. A scan that establishes nothing, or that finds only an `xcodebuild` build naming the simulator, lets the run continue, and the check at attach still applies. QaReN does not stop the driver or restart the device. Runs with `--fresh-install` or the iOS device lifecycle flags keep their own strict admission, unchanged.
